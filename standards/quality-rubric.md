# Quality Rubric | A Tough Editor, Not a Fake Oracle

## Purpose
Create a repeatable way to decide whether writing deserves publication, revision, or rejection. Scores are editorial judgments, not scientific estimates of virality and not a substitute for human taste.

## Hard veto first
Apply standards/anti-patterns.md. If any hard veto appears, mark REJECT even if the lines are clever.

## Six scores, 1 to 5 each
| Dimension | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Emotional impact | inert | some feeling | distinct, strong, earned feeling |
| Psychological precision | vague | recognizable | sharply observed behavior or tension |
| Originality | cliche | familiar but refreshed | particular and genuinely surprising |
| Literary execution | clumsy or formulaic | clean | remarkable rhythm, compression, and landing |
| Shareability | little social use | could save or share | immediate plausible reason to send or repost |
| Brand fit | anonymous quote page | adjacent | unmistakably raw, human, alive, RAISE HELL |

## The concept and brand gates
Before adding the six scores, ask whether the *underlying premise* is worth publishing and whether the copy belongs unmistakably to RAISE HELL.

- **Originality:** Identify the exact idea a person has not heard articulated this way. A fresh simile attached to conventional advice is not a fresh idea.
- **Brand fit:** Look for appetite for life, nerve, an earned point of view and personality. This does not mandate aggression; a devastatingly tender post can be unmistakably RAISE HELL.
- **Emotional force:** Is the reader likely to feel a distinct reaction beyond mild agreement: a jolt, laugh, pang, relief or urge to act? This is an editorial judgment, not a predicted metric.
- **Form:** Does the length, rhythm and final beat suit this particular thought, or has it been poured into a three-paragraph motivational template?

A candidate with **Originality or Brand fit below 3/5 should be rebuilt or rejected regardless of its aggregate score**. Do not game the score by relabeling mild reassurance as Swagger or simple instructions as Fire. No score overrides owner approval.

## Initial editorial thresholds
- **26-30:** strong candidate for human review, subject to vetoes and batch fit.
- **25:** meets the initial target; verify that the overall effect is greater than the component scores.
- **20-24:** promising but revise, or consider exceptional context.
- **Below 20:** rebuild the idea or reject.

**25/30** is a calibration starting point, not a universal rule. Do not inflate scores to hit quota. A surprising post might deserve consideration despite a rubric weakness; explain why. A 29 can still be boring if all six scores are generous.

## Minimum bar
A publishable post needs:
1. A specific observation, meaningful idea, or felt truth.
2. A legitimate emotional payoff.
3. Its own voice, not just a rearranged familiar phrase.
4. No prohibited framing.
5. Readability on the intended visual canvas.
6. Explicit owner approval.

## Evidence-based scoring
For each candidate, identify exact words causing impact or weakness. Never write "5/5 shareability because viral" without evidence. Shareability is a hypothesis until measurable performance exists.

## Batch quality matters independently
Before presenting ten candidates, look at the ten together. Check register, topic, architecture, number of paragraphs, ending function, implied speaker, and intellectual surprise. A set of ten with the same safe psychological-reveal-and-advice *idea architecture* **fails batch review**, even when each individual item is reasonably written. Similar paragraph counts or narrative forms are acceptable when the ideas themselves differ and are strong. Regenerate weak premises and forms instead of merely changing punctuation.

Especially when the requested direction is Mixed, look for real Fire and Swagger alongside Heart, Truth and Perspective. Variety is about thought and emotional stakes, not rotating tags or forms to meet a quota. Use [Editorial Gold](editorial-gold.md) as the positive quality benchmark, not as a bank of ideas to paraphrase.

## Adversarial editorial questions
- Is the first sentence the first true thing, or throat-clearing?
- Did we rely on a recycled viral format?
- Is anything here only for the aesthetic of profundity?
- Does this actually change or clarify a feeling?
- Would the reader be proud to share it?
- Is it too similar to the last five candidates?
- Is the *idea* extraordinary, or just the phrasing competent?
- Is the ending adding impact or explaining a lesson the reader already understood?
- Would it still have a distinctive voice without the graphic design?
- Is there a shorter version that is better? Or a longer, more human one?
- If you stripped all swearing, what remains?
- Would a strong editor still like this tomorrow?

## Decision labels
**REJECT:** hard veto or weak premise. **REVISE:** worthwhile idea, insufficient execution. **CANDIDATE:** ready for human selection, not yet approved. **APPROVED:** only after human approval. **PUBLISHED:** only after actual posting. **GOLD STANDARD:** only after owner-selected real breakout validation.

Never advance content between these states by assertion alone.

## What a failed batch can look like
A first live test produced ten tidy three-paragraph posts, predominantly gentle interpersonal and psychological observations. The individual work was often humane, but the aggregate effect was predictable, insufficiently daring, and too close to an advice account. Do not assign high Brand fit just because a post is compassionate or has a good ending. The diagnostic batch is **not** a set of Gold Standards.

## Source-of-truth examples
The primary positive literary reference is [Editorial Gold](editorial-gold.md), a curated set of untested creative benchmarks. Do not call them published or performance-proven. Reject derivative copy that merely echoes their moves, even when the original examples are excellent.

## Calibration
As owner feedback and performance data arrive, keep human qualitative judgments alongside quantitative outcomes. Record disagreements. A post that underperformed may still be good writing; one that went viral may reveal a mechanism that we do not want to repeat.
