# Language and Style | Spoken, Sharp, Alive

## Primary register
Contemporary plain English. Natural contractions, concrete verbs, and language a person would actually say. Profanity can appear in an authentic register. Do not write like corporate copy, a Zen poster, a screenplay trailer, or an AI pretending to be a literary giant.

## Sentence design
- Prefer direct verbs over nominalizations: "You waited" beats "Your period of delayed action."
- Mix hard stops with longer breaths. There is no mandated three-line pattern.
- Never require every sentence to be short. A flowing, searching sentence may fit an intimate idea.
- Use second person often, not always. First-person plural can feel conspiratorial; third person can add distance or humor.
- Concrete nouns frequently improve force: voicemail, bus stop, birthday candle, suitcase. Do not randomly sprinkle imagery.

## Length
- Short is a feature but not a fetish.
- A single sentence can work.
- Two to four short paragraphs can work.
- Longer copy is permitted if every progression earns its space and fits the visual format.
- No universal word limit as literary doctrine. The renderer will separately establish readable character density for each format. Don't shrink typography into illegibility to preserve a weak extra line.

## Profanity
Use it when it changes cadence or truthfulness. Ordinary usage can include "fuck," "shit," "hell," and their natural forms. Avoid stacked profanity, slurs, demeaning epithets, or edgy shock simply to seem fearless. If "fucking" is the only surprising word, the draft is too bland.

## Punctuation and case
- Periods, commas, colons, semicolons when earned.
- Avoid em dashes.
- Ellipses sparingly, not as suspense machinery.
- Exclamation marks sparingly, only for an actual exclamation.
- All caps only when truly needed for a voice beat, never as a baseline aesthetic.
- No hashtags or username in the actual post body by default.
- No forced emoji punctuation inside literary copy.

## Line breaks
A paragraph break is a breath or a turn. Do not break after every sentence to simulate importance. Do not trap the final word alone unless it genuinely improves delivery. The writing should still work as plain paragraphs.

## Lexical watchlist
Avoid or strongly challenge:
- "Unleash your potential," "embrace the journey," "level up," "grindset," "be the best version of yourself."
- "Wolves," "sheep," "lions," "kings," "empires," "warriors," "alphas," as lazy personal-worth metaphors.
- "They laughed at you," "prove them wrong," anonymous haters, manufactured enemies.
- "You are exactly where you need to be," "everything happens for a reason," guaranteed success.
- Abstract word piles: "authenticity, greatness, resilience, legacy."
- Generic AI transitions: "Here's the truth," "The reality is," "Let that sink in" as reflexive hooks.
- Repeated contrast formulas: "It's not about X. It's about Y" in post after post.

These are signals to inspect, not a blanket ban on every common word.

## Precision and humility
Don't pretend universal laws of psychology are facts. Prefer "sometimes," "a lot of us," or a more specific situation when warranted. Don't force qualifiers into an emotionally clear line merely to sound cautious. Be confident where it is reasonable and avoid false certainties where it is not.

## Originality
Write afresh rather than paraphrasing a known viral quote. Strong phrases in the reference corpus are calibration signals, not copy sources. A familiar thought can become original through a particular new observation.

## Screen test
Read the proposed copy as if it were the only thing on a black square. Would it hold without a soundtrack, picture, caption, author reputation, or comment prompt? If not, return to the insight.
